\paragraph{The coupled point model against the point model alone.}
With uniform fields, $c=1$ everywhere and the antibiotic $a=1$ from the start, every point model in the
coupled run must follow the point model alone. Fritsch et al.'s synthetic case~I
\cite{Fritsch2025BayesianMicrofilms} run this way agrees with the point model alone to $2\times10^{-14}$
(Python, with the Fortran point model of the ANSYS runs and with an independent implementation in JAX,
which agree with each other to $2\times10^{-16}$). In ANSYS every step of the traced Gauss points is
recomputed from its start state and its inputs $(c_n,c_{n+1},a_n,a_{n+1})$; they agree to
$1.8\times10^{-12}$ in every step, once Newton's method is iterated to its tolerance: the first step from
the start state needs up to 38 iterations, a fixed budget of 6 or 12 leaves it unconverged. The constraint
$\sum_i\phi_i+\phi_0=1$ of the point model holds to $2\times10^{-16}$ at every traced step.
The hand-over was checked separately, because the replay above sees only the point model: the living volume
fraction that the field equations use equals the one the point model returned in the same increment, at every
increment (difference $0$), and the start state of each step is the end state of the step before.

\paragraph{Two examples.}
Following the partner's suggestion, two examples use the same model with different start values
(synthetic data, case~I of Fritsch et al., two species, $b=(1,2)$): species A in the seed of this chapter
and species B in four small blocks at the corners, and both species mixed in the seed in equal amounts
($\phi_1=\phi_2=0.275$, the total of Fritsch et al.). The nutrient is held on the layer $y=-1$~mm, the
antibiotic on all outer faces from $T^*=0.5$, with $\alpha^*_0=50$ and a weaker uptake than the nutrient
($g_a\ell^2/d_a=0.25$ against $4$; an assumption). The front term uses a tenth of the converted Table~2 value
of $r$; with the full value the elements collapse within the first increments. A point starts its point model
when $\phi_{3D}$ first exceeds $\phi_{\min}=0.1$. The papers do not say from which composition it starts
(Section~\ref{sec:ch5-outlook-python}); here it starts as species B within $0.5625$~mm of the vertical edges
and as species A elsewhere in the first example, and from the mixed start in the second. This is an
assumption: it fixes where the two regions meet. Starting instead from the mean state of the colonised
neighbours changes the living volume fraction of the seed by $0.025$ (Python).
Table~\ref{tab:ch5-living} gives the seed and the corner blocks at $T^*=1$.

\paragraph{Where the two regions meet.}
Figure~\ref{fig:ch5-contact} follows the separate example without antibiotic to $T^*=2$ (Python, $32^3$, Eq.~35
with its time derivative). The regions of A and B grow until their fronts meet about $0.8$~mm from the centre of
the seed along the diagonal, and the boundary stays there: its position on the diagonal is $0.795$~mm at
$T^*=1$, $1.5$ and $2$, and the share of A drops from above $0.95$ to below $0.05$ within one cell. The
composition is not carried with the biomass in this coupling: a point keeps the state of its point model, and a
point the biofilm reaches starts from the state of its neighbours. Neither species can therefore invade the
other; mixing is confined to the points that start between the two fronts. With the start rule of the ANSYS runs
(species B near the edges, A elsewhere) the boundary has the shape set by the rule instead of the shape of the
meeting fronts, at the same position on the diagonal. The same holds with the antibiotic: in the corner blocks the living
volume fraction of B falls from $0.84$ at $T^*=0.5$ to $0.37$ at $T^*=2$, while the share of A there stays between
$0.02$ and $0.03$ and the boundary stays at $0.796$~mm. A sharp boundary between colonies that meet is what
experiments show as well; that it cannot move, even when one side dies, is a limit of this coupling.
Displacing one species by the other would need the species to be transported in the field equations, as in a
species-resolved continuum model \cite{Feng2021SymbioticBiofilm}.

\paragraph{What the antibiotic does.}
Species B, the more sensitive one ($b_2=2$), dies wherever the antibiotic arrives. In the mixed seed the
living volume fraction falls to $0.23$ and the nutrient, no longer consumed, rises from $0.46$ to $0.84$.
In the corner blocks of species B it falls from $0.75$ to $0.33$. Whether a species survives depends on the
balance in its equation for $\psi_i$ between the nutrient term $c^*\phi_i\sum_jA_{ij}\phi_j\psi_j$ and the
antibiotic term $\alpha^*b_i\psi_i$: with $c^*=100\,c$ and $\alpha^*b_1=\alpha^*_0$, species A gains where
$c>\alpha^*_0/100$. In the separate seed ($c\approx0.5$) the living volume fraction at $T^*=1$ follows
$\alpha^*_0$: $0.94$, $0.90$, $0.77$ and $0.59$ for $\alpha^*_0=10$, $40$, $50$ and $60$ ($8^3$ [$16^3$: ..]). The nutrient field
thus decides where the antibiotic acts, which a point model with a constant $c^*$ cannot show.

The mixed example also depends on the start: with the start of Fritsch et al.\ ($\phi_1=0.25$,
$\phi_2=0.30$) the living volume fraction in the seed ends at $0.051$, with equal amounts at $0.23$ (ANSYS
$16^3$; Python $0.050$ and $0.21$). As in case~6 of Section~\ref{sec:ch5-outlook-python}, the start share decides
which species prevails.

The growth $\alpha-1$ and the stress change little with the antibiotic: in the mixed seed the stress is
$1.18\cdot10^{-3}$~Pa without and $1.27\cdot10^{-3}$~Pa with it ($+8\,\%$), through the nutrient left by the
dead biomass and the front term; $\alpha-1$ changes by $0.8\,\%$. Eq.~36 is unchanged, so the biofilm expands
with $\phi_{3D}$ whether it is alive or dead, and the point model reaches the stress only through the
consumption, the nutrient field and the front term of Eq.~34.

\paragraph{Convergence and sensitivity.}
Halving the outer increment twice ($0.025$, $0.0125$, $0.00625$) changes the seed stress by $0.55$ and
$0.31\,\%$ (first order; the error at $0.025$ is about $0.9\,\%$) and the living volume fraction of the
corner blocks by $3.5\,\%$ ($8^3$); on $16^3$ halving it changes the seed stress by $0.25\,\%$. The Python
version changes by less than $0.5\,\%$.

\paragraph{Limitations of the discretisation.}
On $8^3$ the field $\phi_{3D}$ leaves $[0,1]$ slightly at the edge of the seed early in the run ($-0.027$ and
$1.013$ at $T^*=0.25$, inside the bounds from $T^*=0.75$ on; not on $16^3$): the operator of the element is not
monotone on the coarse mesh. The examples are mirror-symmetric in $x$ and $z$, the results are not: with only
the diffusion of $\phi_{3D}$, $\phi_{3D}$ differs by up to $0.0085$ between mirrored elements on $8^3$, and in
the runs of Section~[..] on $16^3$ by up to $0.9\,\%$ in $x$. The neighbour search of the element enlarges its
radius until it holds enough points and then takes a fixed number of them in element order, so the last
distance shell is cut by the numbering at every point. A rebuild of the operator in Python on the same mesh
shows that this rule alone produces an asymmetry of this size; taking more neighbours or the nearest ones
reduces it, and the mean of $\phi_{3D}$ in the seed changes by $0.5\,\%$ between these choices. All runs keep
the element's setting. With $120$ neighbours the mixed example with antibiotic on $16^3$ changes its seed
living volume fraction from $0.226$ to $0.224$ and its seed stress by $+4.8\,\%$. Small asymmetries of
$\phi_{3D}$ ($0.04$ at most on $16^3$) grow in the point model where it lies near the threshold of its
outcome: the living volume fraction and the share of A differ by up to $0.3$ between mirrored elements at the
edge of the biofilm, while the stress differs by less than $2\,\%$.
