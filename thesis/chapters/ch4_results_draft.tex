\paragraph{The coupled point model against the point model alone.}
With uniform fields, $c=1$ everywhere and the antibiotic $a=1$ from the start, every point model in the
coupled run must follow the point model alone. Fritsch et al.'s synthetic case~I
\cite{Fritsch2025BayesianMicrofilms} run this way agrees with the point model alone to $2\times10^{-14}$
(Python, with the Fortran point model of the ANSYS runs and with an independent implementation in JAX,
which agree with each other to $2\times10^{-16}$). In ANSYS every step of the traced Gauss points is
recomputed from its start state and its inputs $(c_n,c_{n+1},a_n,a_{n+1})$; they agree to
$1.8\times10^{-12}$ in every step, once Newton's method is iterated to its tolerance: the first step from
the start state needs up to 38 iterations, a fixed budget of 6 or 12 leaves it unconverged.

\paragraph{Two examples.}

(synthetic data, case~I of Fritsch et al., two species, $b=(1,2)$): species A in the seed of this chapter
and species B in four small blocks at the corners, and both species mixed in the seed. The nutrient is
held on the face $y=-1$~mm, the antibiotic on all outer faces from $T^*=0.5$, with $\alpha^*_0=50$ and a

\paragraph{What the antibiotic does.}
Species B, the more sensitive one ($b_2=2$), dies wherever the antibiotic arrives. In the mixed seed the
living volume fraction falls from $0.90$ to $0.07$ by $T^*=0.75$, and the nutrient, no longer consumed,
rises from $0.39$ to $0.88$. Species A in the separate seed loses part of its living biomass ($0.96$ to
$0.57$). Whether a species survives depends on the balance in its equation for
$\psi_i$ between the nutrient term $c^*\phi_i\sum_jA_{ij}\phi_j\psi_j$ and the antibiotic term
$\alpha^*b_i\psi_i$: with $c^*=100\,c$ and $\alpha^*b_1=50$, species A grows where $c>0.5$ and declines where $c<0.5$.
The seed lies near this threshold: $c=0.475$ in ANSYS ($8^3$) and $0.533$ in Python ($32^3$), and the living
fraction of A at $T^*=1$ is $0.57$ and $0.81$.
The nutrient field thus decides where the antibiotic acts, which a point model with a constant $c^*$
cannot show. Through the consumption [..] the nutrient left by the dead biomass [..] the spreading and
the stress [..].

\paragraph{Python against ANSYS.}
The Python version with the boundary conditions of the ANSYS deck (the nutrient and the antibiotic held on
whole element layers) gives in the mixed seed at $T^*=1$ a living fraction of $0.050$ and $c=0.937$ (ANSYS
$0.048$ and $0.907$), in the separate seed $0.805$ and $0.533$ (ANSYS $0.567$ and $0.475$). In both the
seed's $\phi_{3D}$ falls below 1 as the front term moves biomass towards the nutrient (Python $0.77$,
ANSYS $0.58$ at $T^*=1$); the remaining difference comes from the discretisation
of the fields ($32^3$ finite volumes against $8^3$ elements of the neighboured element method).
