\chapter{GPU-Accelerated Bayesian Inference for a Five-Species Biofilm}
\label{ch:heine}

This chapter calibrates the point model of Chapter~\ref{ch:theory} on the
in-vitro time courses of a defined five-species oral biofilm of Heine et
al.~\cite{heine2025influence, Heine2025PeriImplant}. The unknowns are the
$15$ entries of the symmetric interaction matrix $\mathbf{A}$. They are
effective parameters of the model, and the main result of the chapter is a
statement, entry by entry, of what the data determine and what they leave
open. The last section carries the same type of model to sparse in-vivo data
with a metabolic sign prior (Section~\ref{sec:heine_signprior}), and
Chapter~\ref{ch:ansys} brings the point model into a finite element framework.

\medskip\noindent\textit{This chapter is based on the manuscript}~\cite{NishiokaEML2026}:
Nishioka K., Klempt F., Geisler H., Mukherjee R., Heine N., Doll-Nikutta K.,
Stiesch M., Szafra\'{n}ski S.P., Soleimani M., Junker P., ``Identifiability of
species interactions in a living material: Bayesian calibration of an extended
Hamilton principle multiphase model'' (in preparation for \textit{Extreme
Mechanics Letters}). All runs of this chapter, with their configurations,
convergence records and posterior samples, are archived; an earlier set of
calibrations from March and April 2026, whose configurations were not kept, is
not used.

%==============================================================================
\section{Experimental System}
\label{sec:heine_data}
%==============================================================================

Heine et al.~\cite{heine2025influence} cultivated a defined consortium of five
oral taxa, \textit{Streptococcus oralis} (\textit{So}),
\textit{Actinomyces naeslundii} (\textit{An}), \textit{Veillonella}
(\textit{Vei}), \textit{Fusobacterium nucleatum} (\textit{Fn}) and
\textit{Porphyromonas gingivalis} (\textit{Pg}), from early colonisers to the
late anaerobic pathogen. The experiment crosses the \emph{community}
(commensal or dysbiotic) with the \emph{cultivation} (static or the HOBIC flow
reactor), which gives the four conditions used throughout this thesis:
Commensal Static (CS), Commensal HOBIC (CH), Dysbiotic Static (DS) and
Dysbiotic HOBIC (DH). The two communities differ not only in their ecological
state but also in the \textit{Veillonella} species (\textit{V.~dispar} and
\textit{V.~parvula}) and in the \textit{Pg} strain (DSM\,20709 and W83).

The composition data are the species fractions of viable cells at days $3$, $6$,
$10$, $15$ and $21$, measured by quantitative real-time PCR after propidium
monoazide (PMA) treatment, and the total-count fractions without PMA measured on
the same samples. I use the measured replicate values provided by the authors
(three to five biological replicates per time point, each in technical
triplicate) and the medians over them. Values read off the published box plots
deviate from these by up to $0.42$ in a species fraction in the dysbiotic
conditions, so they are not used. The measured data are available from the
authors on request and are not reproduced in this thesis; only summary numbers
and figures are shown.

The day-$1$ composition, the earliest measurement, is the initial state of the
model at $t=0$. Model time is the time since inoculation, $t=s\,d$ with
$s=0.2375/21$; in the flow reactor the inoculated medium is replaced after
$24$\,h, so the growth phase there can also be taken to start at day~$1$. The
viable and the total-count compositions differ (RMSE between their medians
$0.101$, $0.087$, $0.119$ and $0.087$ in CS, CH, DS and DH) because the species
differ in viability, so each is compared with its own model counterpart.

The fraction of membrane-intact cells from SYTO9/propidium iodide staining is
image based and \emph{not} resolved by species; per time point it is the mean
over $15$--$45$ confocal images, five per biofilm. It enters the inference as the
community live fraction. The pH, measured in the flow reactor, is not used in
the inference and serves as a check (Section~\ref{sec:heine_validation}).

%==============================================================================
\section{Inference Setup}
\label{sec:heine_setup}
%==============================================================================

The forward model is the coupled $\bphi$--$\bpsi$ system of
Section~\ref{sec:theory_hamilton}, integrated by implicit Euler with Newton's
method ($2{,}500$ steps over the $21$ days) with a barrier term that diverges at $0$ and $1$ and keeps
$\phi_i$ and $\psi_i$ inside $(0,1)$. Only the product $c^*a_{ij}$ enters, so
$c^*=25$ fixes the scale (Section~\ref{sec:theory_replicator}); the assays
contain no antibiotic, so $\alpha^*=0$.

\subsection{The 15-parameter interaction matrix}
\label{sec:heine_matrix}

Only the symmetric part of $\mathbf{A}$ enters the quadratic free energy, so
$\mathbf{A}$ is symmetric with $15$ independent entries $a_{ij}$, $i\le j$. Each
coefficient is an effective, undirected interaction of the model: it lumps
mechanisms the model does not resolve, such as metabolite exchange,
co-aggregation or the pH. Table~\ref{tab:heine_network} lists the interactions
documented for this consortium. All $15$ entries are estimated, none is set to
zero, and agreement with the table is read as consistency with the literature,
not as the measurement of a mechanism.

\subsection{Likelihood}
\label{sec:heine_likelihood}

The viable fractions $y^{\mathrm{v}}_{ik}$ at time $t_k$ are compared with the
living volume fractions $\hat y^{\mathrm{v}}_{ik}=\phi_i\psi_i/\sum_j\phi_j\psi_j$,
the total-count fractions $y^{\mathrm{t}}_{ik}$ with
$\hat y^{\mathrm{t}}_{ik}=\phi_i/\sum_j\phi_j$, and the community live fraction
$\ell_k$ with $\hat\ell_k=\sum_i\phi_i\psi_i/\sum_i\phi_i$, through the weighted
Gaussian log-likelihood
\begin{equation}
  \ln p(\mathbf{y}_{\mathrm{obs}}\mid\btheta)
  =-\frac12\sum_{k}\sum_{i=1}^{5}w_{ik}\left[
  \frac{\bigl(y^{\mathrm{v}}_{ik}-\hat y^{\mathrm{v}}_{ik}\bigr)^2}{(\sigma^{\mathrm{v}}_{ik})^2}
  +\frac{\bigl(y^{\mathrm{t}}_{ik}-\hat y^{\mathrm{t}}_{ik}\bigr)^2}{(\sigma^{\mathrm{t}}_{ik})^2}\right]
  -\frac{\lambda_\psi}{2}\sum_k\frac{(\ell_k-\hat\ell_k)^2}{\sigma_\ell^2}
  +\mathrm{const}.
  \label{eq:heine_lik}
\end{equation}
The standard deviations $\sigma^{\mathrm{v}}_{ik}$ and $\sigma^{\mathrm{t}}_{ik}$ are
estimated from the spread of the measured replicates (interquartile range divided
by $1.35$, at least $0.05$), and $\sigma_\ell$ is the standard deviation of the
live fraction between biofilms, averaged over the time points. The weights
$w_{ik}$ act on both compositions alike: they up-weight \textit{Pg}
($\lambda_{\mathrm{Pg}}=5$), whose late increase is the main dynamic feature of the
dysbiotic data, and the last two time points ($\lambda_{\mathrm{late}}=3$), and
down-weight species with a mean fraction below $5\,\%$ ($\lambda_{\mathrm{rare}}=0.1$).
The live fraction is weighted by $\lambda_\psi=1.5$ in the commensal conditions,
$2.0$ in DS and $3.0$ in DH. The pH does not enter.

In stages (i) and (ii) below, the viabilities are not evolved but fixed to the
values implied by the two compositions and the live fraction,
$\psi_i(t_k)=(y^{\mathrm{v}}_{ik}/y^{\mathrm{t}}_{ik})\,\ell_k$, clipped to
$[0.01,0.99]$ (the community value $\ell_k$ for species below $2\,\%$ of the total
count), and the live-fraction term is omitted. These fixed values are uncertain
(the median width of their $90\,\%$ intervals is $0.57$), so they serve only to
start the inference and as a check, not as data in the likelihood. In stages
(iii) and (iv), $\psi_i$ follows Eq.~\eqref{eq:gov_psi} and all three terms are
used.

\subsection{Prior and four stages}
\label{sec:heine_twophase}

The prior is uniform on a box $[l_k,u_k]$ for each entry. The first boxes were
set from earlier calibrations of a previous version of the model on the same
data, and some of them exclude negative values or allow only small ones, so the
signs of these entries are partly set by the prior in the first two stages;
all of these bounds are widened in stage~(iii). Inference proceeds in four
stages on the same data (Figure~\ref{fig:heine_pipeline}):
(i)~the two composition terms with $\psi_i$ fixed to the data, on the box;
(ii)~as (i), on the box narrowed to the stage-(i) posterior mean $\pm4$ standard
deviations;
(iii)~the full likelihood with $\psi_i$ evolved, back on the full box, half of the
initial particles drawn around the stage-(ii) MAP and half from the prior;
(iv)~as (iii), on the box narrowed to the stage-(iii) posterior mean $\pm4$
standard deviations.
A $\pm4$ standard deviation box removes less than $10^{-4}$ of a Gaussian
marginal, so narrowing shortens the tempering without truncating the posterior.
If more than $20\,\%$ of the posterior mass of an entry lies in the outer
$5\,\%$ of the box on one side, the bound is moved outwards and the stage is
repeated.

  point carries the volume fractions $\phi_i$ and viabilities $\psi_i$ of five

\subsection{TMCMC on the GPU and acceptance criteria}
\label{sec:heine_gpu}

TMCMC~\cite{ChingChen2007TMCMC, DelMoral2006SMC} passes a particle population
through the tempered posteriors $p_m(\btheta)\propto p(\mathbf{y}_{\mathrm{obs}}\mid
\btheta)^{\beta_m}p(\btheta)$, $0=\beta_0<\dots<\beta_K=1$, choosing each increment
so that the coefficient of variation of the importance weights stays at a target
value~\cite{Betz2016TMCMC}. After resampling, every particle performs
$n_{\mathrm{mut}}$ Metropolis--Hastings steps, with random-walk proposals scaled by
the weighted particle covariance alternating with differential-evolution
proposals~\cite{terBraak2006DEMC}; proposals outside the box are rejected without
solving the model. A run needs $10^5$ to $3\times10^7$ model solutions. I compile
the implicit solver, with the Jacobian assembly and the linear solve, into a
single XLA kernel with JAX and evaluate all particles at once with
\texttt{vmap}. On one GPU this is $170$ times faster per tempering step than a
12-core CPU at matched settings, and $147$ times end to end
(Table~\ref{tab:heine_timing}), which makes it affordable to repeat every stage
with three seeds. General gradient-based samplers (HMC/NUTS) were not used.

Every stage is run with three independent seeds and accepted only if
(a)~the stored log-likelihood of every particle agrees with a recomputation;
(b)~the tempering reaches $\beta=1$;
(c)~each particle is moved at least twice per tempering step and at least five
times per free parameter over the run;
(d)~the maximum log-likelihood differs by at most $1$ across seeds; and
(e)~every posterior median differs across seeds by at most half a posterior
standard deviation. An entry whose pooled posterior standard deviation is at
least $0.8$ times that of a uniform distribution on the prior box is reported as
\emph{not identified} and excluded from (e); for an entry with several posterior
modes, (e) is applied within each mode. All other entries are called
\emph{identified}: the data constrain them beyond their prior box. Stages
(ii)--(iv) that fail are repeated with more particles or mutation steps and are
not interpreted. Stage~(i) is exploratory: it sets the box of stage~(ii) and
gives the evidence estimates, which are read only as indications.

\subsection{Fit and convergence}
\label{sec:heine_fit}

% Numbers: thesis/HANDOFF_EML_2026-10-19.md and thesis/EML_numbers_2026-10-19.md (final EML runs, 19 Oct 2026).
Stages (ii)--(iv) of CS and CH and stages (iii)--(iv) of DS meet the criteria
(a)--(e) with three seeds each; DS stage~(ii) fails (d), because one seed found a
higher mode (spread of the maximum log-likelihood $1.70$). In DH, stage~(iii) meets them, but at stage~(iv) the seeds settle in
different posterior modes: criterion~(e) fails, with the medians of $a_{22}$
differing by $0.79$ posterior standard deviations. The DH results are therefore
taken from stage~(iii), and this split is discussed as a limitation in
Section~\ref{sec:heine_discussion}. At the final stage the MAP reproduces the
viable composition with an RMSE of $0.087$ (CS), $0.059$ (CH), $0.058$ (DS) and
$0.073$ (DH), the total-count composition with $0.039$, $0.078$, $0.080$ and
$0.159$, and the community live fraction with $0.23$, $0.12$, $0.09$ and $0.07$
(Figure~\ref{fig:heine_fits}, Table~\ref{tab:heine_rmse}). Accepting a stage often
needed more than one attempt: posterior mass accumulated at a bound of the
initial box in every condition and was released by widening that bound, and the
dysbiotic conditions needed more particles. Without the criteria, several of
these runs would have been read as converged.

\subsection{What the data determine}
\label{sec:heine_rewiring}

Figure~\ref{fig:heine_ident} and Table~\ref{tab:heine_ident} classify every entry
of $\mathbf{A}$ at the final stage; Figure~\ref{fig:heine_posteriors} shows the
marginal posteriors. An entry is identified when its posterior standard
deviation is below $0.8$ times that of a uniform distribution on the final box;
otherwise it is determined in sign only (s.o.) when its $90\,\%$ interval
excludes zero, and not identified (n.i.) when it does not. The number of
identified entries is $5$, $6$, $9$ and $8$ in CS, CH, DS and DH. Some entries
are bounded on one side only: $a_{33}$ in CS lies below $-5.3$, and in DS $a_{44}$
and $a_{13}$ have only an upper and $a_{25}$ only a lower bound.

In the commensal conditions only a few entries are identified: five in CS
($a_{11}$, $a_{22}$, $a_{33}$, $a_{13}$, $a_{23}$) and six in CH ($a_{11}$, $a_{12}$,
$a_{22}$, $a_{33}$, $a_{13}$, $a_{23}$), all among the early colonisers
\textit{So}, \textit{An} and \textit{Vei}; $a_{12}$ in CS is determined in sign
only. Where both conditions determine the sign of an entry, it agrees: the
self-interactions of \textit{So} and \textit{Vei} are negative ($a_{11}$,
$90\,\%$ intervals $[-7.0,-1.7]$ in CS and $[-1.4,-0.4]$ in CH; $a_{33}$,
$[-14.6,-5.3]$ and $[-5.8,-0.6]$), and the \textit{So}--\textit{Vei} coefficient
is positive ($a_{13}$, $[+4.7,+11.3]$ and $[+0.3,+2.4]$), in line with the lactate
cross-feeding between streptococci and
\textit{Veillonella}~\cite{Kolenbrander2010OralMultispecies}, although $a_{13}$ is
an effective coefficient and not a measurement of that mechanism. Every entry
involving \textit{Fn} or \textit{Pg} spreads over its whole box. This is
expected: \textit{Fn} and \textit{Pg} stay below $1\,\%$ of the commensal
communities from day~$3$ on, so the data carry little information on their
interactions.

In the dysbiotic conditions, where \textit{Fn} and \textit{Pg} reach
$10$--$27\,\%$ of the community, more entries are identified, now including
interactions of the late colonisers: nine in DS and eight in DH. The
self-interaction of \textit{Fn} is negative in both ($a_{44}$), and the
\textit{An}--\textit{Fn} coefficient is positive in DH ($a_{24}$,
$[+1.3,+8.8]$). The \textit{Vei}--\textit{Pg} coefficient $a_{35}$ is negative
in DH ($[-20.9,-0.6]$) and close to zero in DS ($[-0.34,+0.24]$). The
\textit{So}--\textit{Vei} coefficient, positive in both commensal conditions, is
negative in DS ($a_{13}$, $[-4.9,-2.4]$). The commensal--dysbiotic comparison mixes the
ecological state with the strain differences and the cultivation; the DS--DH
contrast compares the same strains, although the two conditions also differ in
the live-fraction weight and in their prior boxes.

\subsection{The \textit{Fn}--\textit{Pg} coefficient}
\label{sec:heine_fnpg}

The coefficient $a_{45}$ links the bridging organism \textit{Fn} to the pathogen
\textit{Pg}. Comparing the full model with a model in which $a_{45}=0$ gives
$\ln B=-0.06$ (CS), $+0.03$ (CH), $+7.5$ (DS) and $+2.1$ (DH). In the commensal
conditions the data cannot tell the two models apart; in the dysbiotic
conditions they favour a non-zero $a_{45}$. The uniform prior penalises the full
model by the log-width of the $a_{45}$ box, so $\ln B$ is read only within a
condition. At the final stage $a_{45}$ is identified in both dysbiotic
conditions, but its $90\,\%$ intervals include zero ($[-0.3,+2.1]$ in DS,
$[-2.4,+9.2]$ in DH): the coefficient is constrained by the data, but its sign is
not determined.

\subsection{Recovery test}
\label{sec:heine_recovery}

To test the classification, I generated synthetic data with known coefficients
at the measured time points and repeated the inference. The classification of
the $15$ entries as identified or not was recovered for $14$ (CS), $15$ (CH), $14$
(DS) and $15$ (DH) entries. The intervals are less reliable than the
classification: in CH the true value lies inside the $90\,\%$ interval for only
$2$ of the $6$ identified entries. The posterior intervals of this chapter are
therefore read as a lower bound on the uncertainty
(Figure~\ref{fig:heine_recovery}).

\section{Checks Against Data Not Used in the Inference}
\label{sec:heine_validation}
%==============================================================================

The pH measured in the flow reactor was not used in the inference
(Figure~\ref{fig:heine_ph}b,c). A linear regression of the measured pH on the
measured species fractions (intercept and four fractions, \textit{Vei} as
reference) is fitted once on the measured data, the continuous pH records of CH
and DH (mean of five reactors, about $14{,}000$ values per condition) with the
composition interpolated between the sampling days ($R^2=0.88$), and then
applied, unchanged, to the posterior predictive
composition, so the check tests the predicted composition, not a model of pH. In
DH the pH predicted in this way follows the measured rise from $6.4$ to $7.0$
($R^2=0.71$, RMSE $0.097$; $85\,\%$ of the measured values lie inside the $90\,\%$
band). In CH it matches the level of the measured pH (RMSE $0.082$) but not its
variation ($R^2<0$; $44\,\%$ inside the band).

The calibrated model can also be used in silico. Removing \textit{Fn} from the
dysbiotic flow-reactor community reduces the late increase of \textit{Pg} to about
a quarter: the ratio of the \textit{Pg} fraction on day~$21$ to day~$15$ falls
from about $12$ to $3.0$ (Figure~\ref{fig:heine_ph}a). Setting only $a_{45}=0$
removes most of the increase, so the prediction rests mainly on the
\textit{Fn}--\textit{Pg} coefficient, and it can be tested directly by a
co-culture without \textit{Fn}. The size of the increase itself is not determined
by the data: the MAPs of the three seeds give ratios of $9.6$ to $18.0$ (measured
$14.9$), and the $90\,\%$ interval spans $0.6$ to $32$. Under static cultivation
the model gives a smaller late increase than measured ($1.4$--$1.5$ against
$2.5$); without \textit{Fn} the ratio is $1.0$, and $a_{45}=0$ leaves it unchanged.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:heine_discussion}
%==============================================================================

\paragraph{What the calibration supports.} The data determine a small part of
the matrix: in the commensal conditions the interactions among the early
colonisers, and in the dysbiotic conditions also some interactions of
\textit{Fn} and \textit{Pg}. The signs that are determined agree with the
documented pathways where both exist, which is consistency, not proof of a
mechanism. The repeated stages with three seeds and the acceptance criteria were
necessary: several runs that looked converged did not meet them.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The likelihood treats the five fractions as independent although they sum to
one, so the posterior intervals are too narrow (Section~\ref{sec:heine_recovery}). The community live
fraction does not determine the species-specific viabilities $\psi_i$ (in the
model they span $0.03$--$0.96$), so the living fractions of individual species
are not predictions of the calibrated model. The interaction coefficients are
effective quantities of this model and data set, and the recovery test shows
that their intervals understate the uncertainty. The commensal--dysbiotic
comparison is confounded by the strain differences. In CS the model cannot match
the composition and the live fraction at the same time. In DH the final stage
split across seeds into different posterior modes, so DH is reported from
stage~(iii), and the size of the late \textit{Pg} increase is not determined by
the data. The calibration covers a material point; the
spatial structure and the mechanics are not part of the inference. Because the
likelihood uses fractions only, the absolute amount of biomass is a prediction of
the model, and its posterior spread has to be carried into the coupling with the
finite element model of Chapter~\ref{ch:ansys}. The five-species runs of
Chapter~\ref{ch:ansys} use the final CH MAP of this chapter.

\section{From In-Vitro to In-Vivo Data: a Metabolic Sign Prior}
\label{sec:heine_signprior}
%==============================================================================
% Condensed 3 Oct 2026 from the former chapter 4 (chapters/ch4_dieckow.tex,
% kept in the tree for its full text and figures). Internship work.

The calibration above rests on rich in-vitro data: five species, six time points,
four controlled conditions. Clinical data are much sparser. This section shows,
in condensed form, how the same type of model can still be identified there when
the signs of the interactions are fixed beforehand from metabolic models. The
work was done during an internship at NIFE together with S.\,P.\ Szafra\'{n}ski
and is summarised here because it completes the calibration side of the
thesis; the full analysis is available as an internal report.

\paragraph{Data.} Dieckow et al.~\cite{dieckow2024structure} characterised
early biofilm formation on implant abutments in $12$ patients. The analysis
uses $10$ of them, each sampled at three weekly time points. The
16S~rRNA amplicon reads were aggregated into $10$ class-level guilds and
normalised to the simplex, $\sum_i\phi_i=1$. With $10$ guilds and three time
points, the first of which is the initial condition, there are $20$ observations per patient against up to $55$ entries of a
symmetric interaction matrix, so the inverse problem cannot be solved without
additional information.

\paragraph{Sign prior.} The prior of Section~\ref{sec:theory_fba} is assembled
in three layers: experimentally documented production and use of metabolites
from eHOMD~\cite{Escapa2018eHOMD} and Szafra\'{n}ski et
al.~\cite{Szafranski2025} ($34$ constrained guild pairs); parsimonious FBA of
one AGORA2 model per guild~\cite{Heinken2023AGORA2} ($35$ pairs); and
community FBA with MICOM~\cite{Diener2020MICOM} ($72$ pairs). The directed
signals are symmetrised for the symmetric model, and the penalty acts only on
coefficients of the wrong sign.

\paragraph{Model and test.} The replicator form of
Section~\ref{sec:theory_replicator} is fitted with one interaction matrix
$\bA$ shared by all patients and a growth vector $\bb^{(p)}$ per patient. It is
tested by leave-one-patient-out cross-validation: $\bA$ is fitted on nine
patients and frozen, the held-out patient's $\bb^{(p)}$ is fitted from the
first week only, and the second and third weeks are predicted.
Table~\ref{tab:heine_signprior} lists the prediction errors, and
Figures~\ref{fig:heine_loo_patients} and~\ref{fig:heine_loo_scatter} show the
predictions of the best model.

\paragraph{Results.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item The sign prior improves the symmetric model step by step, from
        $0.0595$ without prior to $0.0502$ with all three layers. The
        asymmetric gLV with the first two layers is slightly better
        ($0.0490$); its directed coefficients add freedom that the symmetric
        model does not have. The Bayesian gLV collapses to $\bA\approx0$ with
        three time points.
  \item Only the sign carries over from the metabolic models. A prior that
        ties $A_{ij}$ to the magnitude of the predicted fluxes recovers the
        correct sign for only $4$ to $11$ of $70$ pairs. Fitted without any
        prior, the symmetric model still recovers $11$ of the $14$
        cross-feeding signs predicted by AGORA2 ($78.6\,\%$, against $37.7\,\%$
        under a permutation null, $p=4\times10^{-4}$); the asymmetric gLV
        shows no such agreement ($p=0.96$).
  \item On $127$ independent peri-implant samples of Joshi et
        al.~\cite{joshi2025}, a dysbiosis ratio of the guilds ordered by the
        model separates peri-implantitis from health and mucositis
        (Kruskal--Wallis $p<10^{-4}$; Spearman $\rho=0.46$ with severity).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Limits.} With $10$ patients the gain of the community-FBA layer over
the first two is only marginally significant ($p\approx0.07$). The symmetric
matrix cannot represent directed effects, which is where the asymmetric gLV
gains its edge. For the rest of this thesis the result that matters is a
narrower one: the signs of the interaction matrix are the part of the model
that is supported by independent evidence, in vitro and in vivo.
