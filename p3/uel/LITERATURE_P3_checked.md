Net for the question "does growth stop under pressure": (i) tumor spheroids: yes, at about 6-16 kPa (Helmlinger 1997); (ii) E. coli in chambers: growth arrest at the roof at <= ~10 kPa (Chu 2018) vs. forces in the hundreds of kPa before division stops (2024 preprint, unverified publication); (iii) yeast: growth slows at the MPa scale (Delarue 2016, abstract level). No measurement of a stall pressure in an oral or multispecies biofilm was found. The homeostatic p_h of the model remains a parameter, not a literature value, in agreement with the note in LITERATURE_P3.md.

1. Decide between 0.28 kPa (peer-reviewed, Wear) and 380 Pa (thesis) for S. mutans; cite the paper, state SD.
2. Read Paramonova 2009 (J. Dent. Res.) and Wood 2000 (J. Dent. Res.) in full for an oral-biofilm modulus and plaque thickness.
3. Open the 2024 bioRxiv confinement preprint (or its journal version) for the growth-vs-pressure curve.
4. Use Helmlinger 1997 and Chu 2018 as the two bracketing examples of a growth-stalling stress of order 10 kPa, and say that for oral biofilms p_h is not measured.
5. Search for toothbrushing-cessation / plaque regrowth studies with thickness data (not found in this pass).
