\begin{frame}{The coupling: what the element does and what we add}
\small
\textbf{The element (Oliver's, unchanged)} solves the biofilm field of
Klempt et al.\ 2024, Eq.\ 34, at its points:
\[
\dot\varphi-\beta\,\Delta\varphi-k_a\,\alpha_K
+|\nabla\varphi|\,r\,\frac{c}{K+c}\,\mathbf n_\varphi\!\cdot\mathbf n_c=0,
\qquad \dot\alpha_K=k_a\,\varphi\quad\text{(Eq.\ 36)}
\]
\textbf{We add}, at every Gauss point of the material routine:
\begin{itemize}
  \item take $\varphi$ from the field and integrate Eq.\ 36:
        $\alpha_{n+1}=\alpha_n+k_a\,\varphi\,\Delta t$
  \item growth $\mathbf F_g=(1+\alpha)\mathbf I$ $\to$ stress (our verified law)
\end{itemize}
\vspace{2pt}
Parameters: Klempt et al.\ 2024 Table 2, $k_a=10^{-3}$ per unit time $T^*$,
in the field \emph{and} in Eq.\ 36. ANSYS 2022 R2, one-species runs.
\vspace{4pt}
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[node distance=1.2cm, every node/.style={font=\small}]
\node[draw,fill=c3!10,rounded corners] (f) {field $\varphi$ (Eq.\ 34)};
\node[draw,fill=c3!10,rounded corners,right=of f] (a) {$\alpha$ (Eq.\ 36)};
\node[draw,fill=c3!10,rounded corners,right=of a] (s) {$\mathbf F_g$ $\to$ stress};
\draw[-{Latex}] (f) -- (a); \draw[-{Latex}] (a) -- (s);
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Check 1: exact solution, inside ANSYS}
\small Where the field has no gradients, Eq.\ 34/36 reduce to
$\dot\varphi=k_a\alpha_K$, $\dot\alpha_K=k_a\varphi$
$\Rightarrow$ $\varphi=\sinh(k_a t)$, $\alpha_K=\cosh(k_a t)$.
\vspace{2pt}
\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth,height=0.56\textheight,keepaspectratio]{assets/fig1005_exact.png}
\end{center}
\vspace{-4pt}
ANSYS follows the exact solution of the time-stepping scheme:
$\alpha_K$ to $3\times10^{-11}$ (round-off), $\varphi$ to $10^{-7}$
(about $10^{-4}$ relative; this element is not perfectly gradient-free).
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Three things found along the way}
\small
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{The element's growth variable is the mean of the two species fields.}
        With one species, the unused field stays at its start value, so growth is halved.
        $\Rightarrow$ question to Oliver: intended?
  \item \textbf{Start values must satisfy $\varphi_1+\varphi_2\le 1$}
        (the void fraction is $\varphi_0=1-\sum\varphi_i$). The example input starts
        both species at 1 in the seed; for one species we start the second at 0.
  \item \textbf{The first result set is not in equilibrium:} the element returns zero
        stress on its first calls while it sets up its operators. We drop that set.
\end{enumerate}
\vspace{6pt}
\colorbox{c3!12}{\parbox{0.95\linewidth}{\small
Earlier bring-up runs used larger growth rates (0.1, 0.5) to make stresses visible;
all results on these slides use the published values.}}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Two species: composition from the point model (Klempt et al.\ 2026)}
\small
\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.55\textwidth}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Amount} $\varphi_{3D}=\varphi_1+\varphi_2$: from the element's field
        (Klempt et al.\ 2024, unchanged), drives growth (Eq.\ 36)
  \item \textbf{Composition} $\chi_i=\varphi_i/\sum\varphi$ and $\psi_i$: from the

        $\sum\varphi_i=\varphi_{3D}$ (void $\varphi_0=1-\varphi_{3D}$)
  \item Composition does not act back on the stress: in Klempt et al.\ 2024 no
        material parameter depends on the species
\end{itemize}

\begin{frame}{Two modelling assumptions, stated explicitly}
\small
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Time link $s$.} Klempt et al.\ 2024 run to $T^*=1$; Klempt et al.\ 2026 run
        500--1500 steps of $10^{-4}$ in its own unit. No paper links the two.
        The point model advances $s\,\Delta t$ per step; we use $s=0.15$.
        \begin{itemize}
          \item case 6: the winner emerges only for $s\gtrsim0.15$
          \item case 3: $s$ moves $\chi_1$ by 0.03--0.06
        \end{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Packed regions, $\varphi_{\rm cap}=0.9$.} Where the field is fully
        packed ($\varphi_{3D}\to1$) the point model sees at most 0.9; otherwise the
        result depends on the coupling time step. Sensitivity: 0.664--0.668 (case 3).
\end{enumerate}
\vspace{6pt}
Both are one named number with a sensitivity study --- nothing else is tuned;
all other parameters are the published ones.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Earlier work kept in the thesis: curved substrate}
\small A curved \textbf{substrate} (no growth) bonded to a thin \textbf{growth
layer} on top, with our verified growth law.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Bug found:} the layers were joined only at coincident nodes, i.e.\
        along a few node lines; the ``two-lobe bulge'' reported earlier was the
        layer lifting between them
  \item \textbf{Fix:} one shared interface mesh. The bonded layer bulges
        near-uniformly and converges at second order (2.4k / 19k / 150k elements:
        profile error $2.7\,\%\to0.58\,\%$)
  \item \textbf{Consequence:} growth layer $6.3\times$ the substrate von Mises
        (not $15.2\times$); differences between the 4 clinical conditions
        ($0.4\,\%$) are of the order of placement effects $\Rightarrow$ no condition
        effect on stress is claimed
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}
