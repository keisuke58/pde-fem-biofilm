# Chu et al. (2018): what the computed stress is *for*

> Chu EK, Kilic O, Cho H, Groisman A, Levchenko A.
> **Self-induced mechanical stress can trigger biofilm formation in
> uropathogenic *Escherichia coli*.** *Nature Communications* **9**:4087 (2018).
> doi:[10.1038/s41467-018-06552-z](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-06552-z)

Read 2026-09-30. This repository computes growth-induced von Mises stress in an
oral biofilm and compares it across clinical conditions. The question it has
never had a cited answer to is *so what* — why a stress number matters
biologically. This is the answer, and it comes with a threshold.

---

## What they measured

A microfluidic chamber with a **deformable PDMS roof**, calibrated so the roof's
displacement reads out the pressure the colony generates. Colonies fill the
chamber in five to seven hours; the roof then deforms measurably.

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| max pressure generated by confined colonies | **> ~1.5 psi ≈ 10 kPa** (all four strains: *E. coli* JM105 and CFT073, *P. aeruginosa*, *S. cohnii*) |
| force at a ~1 μm² cell footprint | ~1 nN |
| roof deformation at which `rpoH` upregulation becomes detectable | **~0.75 psi ≈ 5 kPa** |
| for comparison: osmotic bursting of eukaryotic cells | 4.1 kPa (red blood cells) – 6.9 kPa (alveolar epithelial) |
| bacterial turgor pressure (upper bound on what restricts growth) | 30–100 kPa |

## The causal chain

1. Confined growth → self-imposed pressure
2. Pressure → **`rpoH` upregulation** (heat-shock sigma factor σ³²), rising as
   much as 100% once the roof deforms
3. `rpoH` → onset of biofilm formation
4. → increased expression of biofilm markers: EPS (concanavalin A) and curli
   (Congo Red)
5. → **antibiotic tolerance**

And a feedback: *"biofilm matrix deposition can be particularly high in areas of
increased mechanical and biological stress, with the deposited matrix further
enhancing the stress levels."*

## Why this matters here, quantitatively

The four MAP von Mises maxima this repository computes
(`tooth_klempt_comparison.json`, mode A) against the ~5 kPa threshold:

| condition | max von Mises | vs ~5 kPa |
|---|---|---|
| commensal / HOBIC | **13.9 kPa** | above |
| dysbiotic / static | **13.8 kPa** | above |
| commensal / static | **9.3 kPa** | above |
| dysbiotic / HOBIC | **2.3 kPa** | **below** |

**The computed stresses straddle a measured biological threshold**, and three of
the four conditions cross it while one does not. That is the strongest available
answer to what the number is for, and it needs no new data.

### What this is not

Say it carefully or it will not survive a question.

- Chu et al. measure **pressure** in confined *E. coli* colonies under a PDMS
  roof. This repository computes **von Mises** in oral biofilm on a tooth.
  Different invariant, different organisms, different confinement. The bridge is
  **order of magnitude**, not equivalence.
- The table above is **maxima**. The means are 0.46–2.36 kPa, mostly below the
  threshold, so the honest claim is about peak stress in the loaded regions.
- **The absolute values hang on `E_SPEC`, and it is uncertain by two orders of
  magnitude.** Soleimani (2019) Table 2 — the source Klempt 2024 cites for its
  own material properties — uses **E = 10 Pa** for biofilm, while this
  repository assumes 10–1000 Pa per species, giving `E_voigt` ≈ 960 Pa
  (commensal) and 500 Pa (dysbiotic): roughly 50–100× stiffer. Since `σ ∝ E`
  (verified: 2× E gives 2.00× σ), at E = 10 Pa the maxima above would be
  **0.02–0.3 kPa**, two orders *below* the threshold rather than straddling it.
  **So the straddle is a property of our E_SPEC choice as much as of the
  biology.** Quote it as a motivating coincidence of scale, never as a result.
  See [`SOLEIMANI2019_NOTES.md`](SOLEIMANI2019_NOTES.md) §5.
- They come from the Abaqus tooth/implant pipeline, whose `*TIE` coverage has
  never been checked — that is what `tier2b_real/tie_coverage_check.py` is for,
  and it has not been run against the real assembly yet.

## It also justifies naming the one-way coupling as a limitation

The feedback quoted above — stress raising matrix deposition, matrix raising
stress — is precisely the stress → growth path the proposal to Oliver defers to
future work. Klempt et al. 2024 neglect it too (§2.4), and Soleimani et al.
(2020) model it. So the limitation can be stated as a known, measured mechanism
that two modelling papers in this lineage also set aside, rather than as an
omission.

## Where to use it

1. **Motivation / introduction** — the reason growth-induced stress is worth
   computing at all, with the ~5 kPa threshold.
2. **Results** — the straddle table, with the caveats above.
3. **Limitations** — the feedback loop, alongside Soleimani et al. (2020).
