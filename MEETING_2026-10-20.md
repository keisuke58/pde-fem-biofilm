## The frame

- The point model (Klempt et al. 2026, with the TMCMC calibration) is a **black box**: do not change its equations or
  the calibration. At every quadrature point of the 3D model it is called with inputs (c*, alpha*, start values) and
  gives outputs (phi_i, psi_i). The work is how the 3D model feeds it and uses its output.
- **The point model keeps its own constraint** sum_i phi_i + phi_0 - 1 = 0 (normalised volume, phi_i are parts of the
  volume). Not phi_0 = 1 - phi_3D: he withdrew what he had written in his mail. phi_0 is the point model's own
  auxiliary void, left as it is.
- 3D model: Felix's two-species element, species 2 set to zero (homogenised biofilm, one phi_3D).

## 3D -> point model

- **c\***: the nutrient c of the 3D field at the quadrature point (from the nodes, like strains from displacements),
  **linearly interpolated in time** between c(t_{i-1}) and c(t_i) over the point model's inner steps. Klempt 2026
  case 3 already has a time-dependent c. Linear interpolation is a start; can be replaced.
- **alpha\* (antibiotic)**: Felix's 3D model has no antibiotic. **Repurpose the second nutrient field** (the one of the
  unused species 2): sources, diffusion, same equation as c. At the point: linear interpolation in time, as c*.
  Implement the antibiotic even without data (no antibiotic in the Heine data; as in Fritsch et al., an outlook);
  in the worst case alpha* = 0.

## Point model -> 3D

- Eq. 34 (phi_3D) unchanged: the gradient term decides where the biofilm spreads, alive or dead.
- Eq. 36 (d alpha/dt = k_alpha phi_3D) unchanged: inflation, not a matter of alive or dead. (He considered my
  growth-from-living-biomass idea "good, similar to what I intended", but would not put it into alpha.)
- **Eq. 35 changed: only living biomass consumes the nutrient**
      dc/dt = d lap c - g phi_3D (sum_{i=1..N} phi_i psi_i) f(c)
  phi_3D scaled down by the living part (the sum is at most 1; phi_0 has no psi and is not alive).
  The paper writes g phi c; the precise form is g phi f(c) (Monod-like); f(c) -> c for the parameter choice used.
  In the thesis write f(c) and say why it reduces to c.
- The same for the antibiotic field (living biomass takes it up; the point model computes the dying through psi,
  which is fed back). When to feed back psi (before or after the step) is mine to decide.

## Decisions after the meeting (the user, 20 Oct)

- Into the thesis, replacing coupling (a); the antibiotic as an outlook with synthetic data only (the calibrated
  point model has b = 0).
- Consumption with sum_i phibar_i = sum_i phi_i psi_i (Klempt 2026 notation, Oliver's form). A normalised share
  sum phi psi / sum phi was considered first and dropped (the user, 20 Oct, later); phibar_i is a volume fraction of the
  normalised point volume (in [0, 1]), so phi_3D sum phibar_i is the living volume fraction in the field.
- Consumption written g phi c in the thesis and the code (the user, 20 Oct, later; Oliver had suggested writing f(c)).
  Klempt 2024 prints g phi (Eq. 35, Eq. 24); this is a confirmed misprint (Meisam 6 Oct: g phi c in the
  implementation; Oliver 20 Oct). The thesis uses g phi c and may say in one sentence that it corrects the misprint.
- The front term of Eq. 34 switched on, so that the nutrient (and through it the point model) reaches growth and
  stress; r from Table 2 converted (10 mm/T* on the 2 mm cube) and r/10.

- The partner element's consumption term was g phi (zeroth order, as printed): a bug of the element, fixed on
  IKMHIWI03 (A-19) to g phi L c. The consumption is g phi c, as decided; the Chapter 4 results are recomputed with
  the new coupling anyway, so nothing else follows from it.
