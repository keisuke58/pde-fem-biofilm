    60  ! attempts without reaching convergence; the next one should probably not   
    61  ! be another blanket face constraint but either a corner-specific   
    62  ! (mid-edge-only) condition, a fundamentally different constraint scheme,   
    63  ! or a second pair of eyes. 
    64  !   
    65  ! WHY THIS CASE 
    66  ! ------------- 
    67  ! t_growth_constrained.dat and t_growth_free.dat are both a UNIT CUBE with a
    68  ! UNIFORM alpha field -- closed-form-checkable, but geometrically nothing   
    69  ! like a tooth surface (see slides_1005.tex, "this cube is a math check, not
    70  ! the biofilm's actual shape"). This deck is one step toward geometric  
    71  ! realism: a curved SUBSTRATE (tooth-surface proxy, no growth) bonded to a  
    72  ! thin GROWTH LAYER (biofilm, alpha != 0) on top of it. 
    73  !   
    74  ! THIS IS NOT A CLOSED-FORM VERIFICATION. Two curved, bonded, differently-  
    75  ! loaded layers have no simple analytic answer the way F=I does. Treat this 
    76  ! as a SMOKE TEST / qualitative capability check: does the two-material,
    77  ! curved-geometry, bonded-interface case (a) build, mesh, and solve without 
    78  ! error, and (b) produce a physically sensible pattern -- growth layer in   
    79  ! compression (it wants to expand and is bonded down), substrate picking up 
    80  ! bending/hoop stress from being pushed on non-uniformly by the curved growth   
    81  ! layer. A real mesh-convergence study (refine ESIZE, check stress  
    82  ! stabilizes) is NOT done here and should precede using this for anything   
    83  ! quantitative -- see README.md.
    84  !   
    85  ! GEOMETRY (cylindrical coords, CSYS,1: X=radius, Y=theta[deg], Z=axial)
    86  !   substrate:    R_IN  = 4.0  ->  R_MID = 4.3   (tooth-proxy, alpha = 0)   
    87  !   growth layer: R_MID = 4.3  ->  R_OUT = 4.4   (biofilm,     alpha = 0.05)
    88  !   axial length LEN = 3.0, arc ARC = 60 deg (an open patch, not a full ring
    89  !   -- a tooth surface is not a closed cylinder, so don't mesh it like one)

    95  !   edges) is traction-free. This is deliberately the crudest BC that still 
    96  !   lets the growth layer push against something -- not meant to model a
    97  !   real periodontal support.

   116  !       volumes 1,2 come out as 1,3; confirmed via out_cyl.txt) and the 
   117  !       glued replacement volume 3 is evidently a NEW entity that does not  
   118  !       inherit volume 2's attribute, so everything silently fell back to   
   119  !       the default material (ESEL,S,MAT,,2 selected zero elements).
   120  !   (2) VATT by centroid radius (CSYS,1, VSEL,LOC,X) after VGLUE -- selected
   121  !       zero volumes too; APDL's LOC selection for a volume apparently  
   122  !       checks its defining keypoints, not a true centroid, and this
   123  !       curved wedge's keypoints sit exactly at R_IN/R_MID/R_MID/R_OUT (the 
   124  !       corners), not at any in-between radius.

   126  ! its own default material pointer (MAT,n before VSWEEP), then bond the two 
   127  ! independently-meshed volumes by merging their coincident interface nodes  
   128  ! with NUMMRG -- ordinary technique for two structured sweep-meshes sharing
